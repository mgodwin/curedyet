# Atrial fibrillation: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

## Sources

- [Wang et al., Front Cardiovasc Med 2025: global burden of AF/flutter 1990-2021 (GBD 2021)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12133759/) - 52.55 million prevalent cases, 4.48 million new cases and 0.34 million deaths worldwide in 2021
- [CJC Open 2024 editorial: updated global perspective of AF](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11963156/) - Higher recorded prevalence in high-SDI countries, faster growth in middle and lower SDI countries, likely underreporting in low-SDI settings
- [CDC: About atrial fibrillation](https://www.cdc.gov/heart-disease/about/atrial-fibrillation.html) - AF causes about 1 in 7 strokes; about fivefold ischemic stroke risk; 12.1 million US cases projected; deaths and hospitalizations
- [Staerk et al., BMJ 2018: lifetime risk of AF (Framingham)](https://www.bmj.com/content/361/bmj.k1453) - Lifetime risk from age 55 of 37.0%; about 1 in 5 with optimal risk factors, over 1 in 3 with at least one elevated risk factor
- [Hart et al., Ann Intern Med 2007: antithrombotic therapy meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17577005/) - Adjusted-dose warfarin cut stroke 64% vs control; antiplatelets 22%
- [Ruff et al., Lancet 2014: DOACs vs warfarin meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24315724/) - DOACs cut stroke or systemic embolism 19% and intracranial bleeding about half vs warfarin, with more GI bleeding
- [Kirchhof et al., NEJM 2020: EAST-AFNET 4](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2019422) - Early rhythm control within a year of diagnosis cut a composite of CV death, stroke, HF or ACS hospitalization (3.9 vs 5.0 per 100 patient-years, HR 0.79)
- [ACC summary of EAST-AFNET 4](https://acc.org/latest-in-cardiology/articles/2020/08/26/15/52/sat-11am-east-afnet-4-early-rhythm-control-therapy-afib-patients-esc-2020) - Trial design and results, 2,789 patients
- [Ganesan et al., JAHA 2013: long-term outcomes of AF ablation](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/JAHA.112.004549) - 19 studies, 6,167 patients, 3+ years: single-procedure freedom 53.1% overall, 54.1% paroxysmal, 41.8% non-paroxysmal; 79.8% with multiple procedures (mean 1.51)
- [Reddy et al., NEJM 2023: ADVENT pulsed field vs thermal ablation (ACC summary)](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Articles/2023/08/23/19/16/sun-555am-advent-esc-2023) - PFA noninferior for paroxysmal AF; 73.3% vs 71.3% one-year success on a composite endpoint; 607 patients
- [Applied Clinical Trials: FDA expands Farapulse approval to persistent AF](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/fda-expands-farapulse-pfa-approval-persistent-af-trial-results) - Persistent AF label July 2025; original paroxysmal approval January 2024
- [Cardiac Rhythm News: ADVANTAGE AF phase 2 meets endpoints](https://cardiacrhythmnews.com/second-phase-of-bostons-advantage-af-study-meets-primary-safety-and-efficacy-endpoints/) - 73.4% 12-month freedom from AF/flutter/tachycardia in persistent AF, 255 patients, 2.4% safety events
- [Kirchhof et al., Europace 2014: PREFER in AF registry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3864758) - AF types: 30.0% paroxysmal, 24.0% persistent, 7.2% long-standing persistent, 38.8% permanent
- [HCPLive: CHAMPION-AF (Mar 2026)](https://www.hcplive.com/view/champion-af-left-atrial-appendage-closure-noninferior-to-noacs) - Watchman FLX noninferior to NOACs at 3 years (5.7% vs 4.8%), less non-procedural bleeding (10.9% vs 19.0%); ischemic stroke 3.2% vs 2.0%
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05757869: LIBREXIA-AF](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05757869) - Milvexian vs apixaban; active, not recruiting; primary completion estimated 2026-12-08 (checked via API 2026-11-05)
- [HCPLive: LIBREXIA-ACS neutral, AF and stroke trials report next year (Aug 2026)](https://www.hcplive.com/view/librexia-acs-fails-to-improve-cardiovascular-outcomes-doesn-t-increase-bleeding-risk) - LIBREXIA-AF results expected 2027
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05712200: LILAC-TIMI 76](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05712200) - Abelacimab vs placebo in AF patients unsuitable for anticoagulation; recruiting; primary completion estimated 2027-12-30; ~2,500 planned (checked via API 2026-11-05)
- [BioSpace: AZALEA-TIMI 71 abelacimab vs rivaroxaban](https://www.biospace.com/study-confirming-overwhelming-reduction-in-bleeding-of-the-dual-acting-factor-xi-xia-inhibitor-abelacimab-as-compared-to-rivaroxaban-selected-as-late-breaker-oral-presentation-at-the-american-heart-association-scientific-sessions) - Phase 2 stopped early for large bleeding reduction vs rivaroxaban
- [PACE-CME: OCEANIC-AF results](https://pace-cme.org/news/results-prematurely-stopped-oceanic-af-trial-asundexian-af/2468011/) - Asundexian was less effective than apixaban at preventing stroke, trial stopped early (a caution for factor XI drugs)
- [Attia et al., Lancet 2019: AI-enabled ECG for AF in sinus rhythm](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31378392/) - AI ECG identifies people with likely AF from a normal-rhythm ECG

## Research log

- 2026-11-05: Baseline research for the first Cure Watch snapshot: toll, ladder levels for paroxysmal and persistent AF, factor XI pipeline dates from ClinicalTrials.gov, ablation and LAAC evidence, forecasts.

## Open questions

- Long-term (5+ year) recurrence after pulsed field ablation is not yet published; the long-term ablation figures used are from older thermal ablation (Ganesan 2013).
- LILAC-TIMI 76 dates conflict: some 2026 trackers list Oct 2026 completion with ~1,900 patients, while ClinicalTrials.gov (updated July 2026) shows Dec 2027 and ~2,500.
- Persistent AF level (2 vs 3) is a judgement call; reconsider if long-term PFA data show durable freedom for a large share.
- CHAMPION-AF figures come from trade coverage and the sponsor; the NEJM paper was not read directly, and an editorial questioned the noninferiority margins.
- OCEANIC-AFINA (asundexian in patients unsuitable for anticoagulants) status and dates were not found on ClinicalTrials.gov.
