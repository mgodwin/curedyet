# Age-related vision loss: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

Scope: the common, near-universal ways vision fades with age, split into presbyopia and cataract. Age-related macular degeneration has its own record (diseases/macular-degeneration); glaucoma is tracked separately.

## Sources

- [WHO fact sheet: Blindness and vision impairment](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/blindness-and-visual-impairment) - at least 2.2B with near or distance vision impairment; 826M unaddressed presbyopia and 94M cataract among about 1B preventable or unaddressed cases; about 1 in 2 people who need cataract surgery can't get it; unaddressed near vision impairment over 80% in parts of sub-Saharan Africa, under 10% in high-income regions
- [Fricke et al., Global prevalence of presbyopia, Ophthalmology 2018 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29753495/) - 1.8B people (25%) with presbyopia in 2015; 826M with near vision impairment from no or inadequate correction; 45% unmet need, greatest in rural low-resource areas
- [GBD 2019 Blindness and Vision Impairment Collaborators, Causes of blindness and vision impairment in 2020, Lancet Global Health 2021 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7820391/) - cataract the leading cause of blindness in adults 50+ in 2020 (15.2M of 33.6M)
- [NEI: Cataracts](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/cataracts) - by age 80 most people have cataracts or have had surgery; surgery is the only way to remove one; very safe; 9 in 10 people see better afterward
- [NEI: Cataract surgery](https://www.nei.nih.gov/learn-about-eye-health/eye-conditions-and-diseases/cataracts/cataract-surgery) - numbing drops, same-day discharge, eye drops while healing, most fully healed by 8 weeks, risks include infection, secondary cataract treated with laser, new glasses prescription after healing
- [Jaycock et al., The Cataract National Dataset electronic multi-centre audit of 55,567 operations, Eye 2009](https://www.nature.com/articles/6703015) - 6/12 or better in 91.0% of all eyes, 94.7% without and 79.9% with co-pathology (UK NHS, 2001-2006)
- [UK multicentre study of Nd:YAG capsulotomy after cataract surgery (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7569058/) - laser for posterior capsule opacification in 3.5% of eyes at 1 year, 8.5% at 2 years
- [Endophthalmitis after cataract surgery, 12-year series (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8007477/) - infection rate about 0.08% overall, falling to 0.026% in 2016-2019; developed-country rates about 0.01-0.05%
- [Daszynski et al., Failure of oxysterols such as lanosterol to restore lens clarity from cataracts, Sci Rep 2019 (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6560215/) - lanosterol did not reverse cataracts or dissolve aggregated protein in human lenses; earlier animal findings not independently confirmed
- [Zhao et al., Lanosterol reverses protein aggregation in cataracts, Nature 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature14650) - the original animal result
- [Ophthalmology Times / PharmTech: FDA approves AbbVie's Vuity (Oct 2021)](https://www.pharmtech.com/view/fda-approves-abbvie-s-vuity-for-presbyopia-treatment) - first presbyopia drop, pilocarpine 1.25%, approved Oct 29, 2021, near vision up to about 6 hours
- [LENZ Therapeutics: FDA approval of Vizz (July 31, 2025)](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/lenz-therapeutics-announces-us-fda-approval-of-vizz-for-the-treatment-of-presbyopia) - aceclidine once-daily drop; onset within 30 minutes, up to about 10 hours
- [The Pharma Letter: FDA approves Tenpoint's Yuvezzi (Jan 2026)](https://www.thepharmaletter.com/pharma-news/fda-approves-tenpoint-s-yuvezzi) - carbachol/brimonidine combination drop, formerly Brimochol PF
- [Pharmaphorum: Novartis drops presbyopia drug acquired with Encore Vision](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/novartis-drops-presbyopia-drug-acquired-with-encore-vision) - UNR844 lens-softening drop discontinued after Phase 2b missed its primary endpoint (2022)
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04806503: UNR844 dose-ranging study](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04806503) - terminated, did not achieve primary objective
- [WHO: Report of the 2030 targets on effective coverage of eye care](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240058002) - WHA74 (2021) targets: +30 percentage points effective cataract surgery coverage, +40 points effective refractive error coverage by 2030

## Research log

- 2026-11-05: New record with two variants, presbyopia (level 2, light) and cataract (level 4, moderate). Disease-level forecast follows presbyopia. Macular degeneration and glaucoma left to their own records.

## Open questions

- **Presbyopia is "Controlled" (2), not "Cured".** Glasses give full near vision with no side effects, so the everyday result is close to cured, but they work only while worn, for life, and the lens keeps stiffening underneath. That matches level 2 ("near-normal life on ongoing treatment"). Drops are the same kind of ongoing workaround, lasting hours. Lens replacement or laser surgery (multifocal implants, monovision) can reduce dependence on glasses, but they trade off contrast, glare or distance vision, are elective and costly, and still don't restore natural focusing, so they don't count as a cure for most.
- **Cataract is "Cured for most" (4), not "Cured for all" (5).** The cloudy lens is removed and can't recur, and about 91% of eyes reach 6/12 or better (about 95% without other eye disease). We read that as clearly over the 85% line but under 98%. The UK audit figures are from 2001-2006; modern outcomes are likely a little better, but we didn't find a newer national headline figure. The 85% line is about cure in rich-country standard care; worldwide only about half of people who need surgery get it.
- **Cataract burden: moderate, not heavy.** METHODOLOGY lists surgery under heavy, but cataract surgery is a short same-day operation under numbing drops, serious infection is around 1 in 2,000 or rarer, and most people are fully healed within 8 weeks, which is far from bowel or brain surgery. We rated it moderate (a real procedure with drops and follow-up visits, plus a small chance of later laser treatment). A reviewer applying "any surgery = heavy" literally would rate it heavy. It isn't light because it is still an operation with a recovery period and rare sight-threatening complications.
- **Disease-level burden light** follows presbyopia (the typical patient). It isn't shown as a verdict because the disease has variants.
- **Cataract reached year 1990 is approximate.** Lens implants and small-incision removal spread through the 1980s; we didn't find a single source dating when it became standard in rich countries, and the cited NEI page supports the cure rate, not the date.
- **Cataract `easy` forecast** assumes mild means non-surgical. No anti-cataract drug is in human trials after lanosterol failed in human lenses, hence low odds. If reviewers decide cataract surgery already counts as near-mild, this would become `reached_easy` instead.
- The ratio uses presbyopia's global prevalence (2015), since it is the condition nearly everyone gets; NEI gives no exact cataract percentage at age 80, only "most".
