# Low back pain: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

Scope: this record covers non-specific low back pain, the roughly 90% of back pain with no specific identifiable cause. Back pain from specific causes (fractures, cancer, infection, inflammatory arthritis such as axial spondyloarthritis, cauda equina syndrome) and sciatica from nerve root compression are different conditions with different treatments, and are not rated here.

## Sources

- [GBD 2021 Low Back Pain Collaborators, Global burden of low back pain 1990-2020 and projections to 2050, Lancet Rheumatology 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10234592/) - 619M prevalent cases in 2020, 843M projected by 2050; leading cause of years lived with disability
- [WHO fact sheet: Low back pain](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/low-back-pain) - about 90% non-specific; acute under 6 weeks, chronic over 12 weeks; painkillers not first line
- [Hartvigsen et al., What low back pain is and why we need to pay attention, Lancet 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29573870/) - no specific cause in nearly all; most episodes resolve but recurrence is common
- [Foster et al., Prevention and treatment of low back pain, Lancet 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29573872/) - guidelines favour education and exercise, psychological programmes for persistent pain, prudent use of drugs, imaging and surgery
- [da Costa et al., The prognosis of acute and persistent low-back pain: a meta-analysis, CMAJ 2012](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3414626/) - pooled pain 52 to 23 to 6 (acute) and 51 to 33 to 23 (persistent) out of 100 at baseline, 6 and 52 weeks
- [WHO guideline for non-surgical management of chronic primary low back pain in adults (2023)](https://www.who.int/news-room/events/detail/2023/12/07/default-calendar/who-guideline-for-non-surgical-management-of-chronic-primary-low-back-pain-in-adults-in-primary-and-community-care-settings) - conditional recommendations incl. exercise, spinal manipulation, NSAIDs; 14 interventions not recommended, including opioids
- [Jones et al., OPAL trial, opioids for acute low back and neck pain, Lancet 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00404-X) - no significant pain benefit over placebo at 6 weeks; more opioid side effects
- [Krebs et al., SPACE randomized trial, JAMA 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29509867/) - opioids not better than non-opioid medicines for pain-related function over 12 months in chronic back, hip or knee pain
- [Ashar et al., Pain reprocessing therapy vs placebo and usual care for chronic back pain, JAMA Psychiatry 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34586357/) - 66% vs 20% vs 10% pain-free or nearly; 151 adults, mean baseline pain 4.1/10
- [Kent et al., RESTORE trial of cognitive functional therapy, Lancet 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00441-5) - large sustained improvements in disability at lower societal cost
- [Cognitive functional therapy relieved chronic disabling low back pain for 3 years (ConsultantLive, RESTORE follow-up in Lancet Rheumatology 2025)](https://www.consultantlive.com/view/cognitive-functional-therapy-relieved-chronic-disabling-low-back-pain-3-years) - benefits sustained at 3 years
- [Pocovi et al., WalkBack trial, Lancet 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38908392/) - walking and education: median 208 vs 112 days to recurrence (HR 0.72)
- [Pharmafile: First European approval for non-opioid treatment for chronic low back pain (VER-01, Jun 2026)](https://pharmafile.com/?p=42943) - German approval; UK MHRA filing targeted for 2026
- [Pharmacy Times: FDA grants Breakthrough Therapy designation to VER-01 (Jun 2026)](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-grants-breakthrough-therapy-designation-to-investigational-cannabis-derived-therapy-for-chronic-low-back-pain) - US Phase 3 data expected 2027, NDA planned 2028
- [Karst et al., VER-01 Phase 3 in chronic low back pain, Nature Medicine 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03977-0) - pain reduction 1.9 vs 1.3 points (difference 0.6 on 0-10); more adverse events than placebo
- [FDA: approval of suzetrigine (Journavx) for moderate to severe acute pain (Jan 2025)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-novel-non-opioid-treatment-moderate-severe-acute-pain)
- [Fierce Biotech: Vertex's suzetrigine sciatica (LSR) Phase 2 result](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/vertexs-pain-trial-hits-endpoint-sinks-stock-placebo-comparison-spooks-investors) - pain change -2.02 vs -1.98 on placebo
- [pharmaphorum: Vertex is smarting from a pain portfolio setback (Aug 2025)](https://pharmaphorum.com/news/vertex-smarting-pain-portfolio-setback) - Vertex will not run another LSR trial after FDA feedback; focus moved to diabetic neuropathy

## Open questions

- What counts as "cured" for a condition that often recurs is a judgement call. Acute is rated 3 (not 4) because recurrence and lingering pain are common; da Costa's pooled pain at one year is low (6/100), so a case for 4 exists if one counts only the current episode.
- The overall level (2) is a blend for anchoring the pooled forecast: acute episodes mostly settle (3), chronic pain is only eased (1). No good single figure gives the share of people with back pain who have chronic pain at a given time.
- Pain reprocessing therapy rests on one single-site trial in people with mostly moderate pain (mean 4.1/10). Larger multi-site replications would be the key event that could lift chronic to 3.
- Suzetrigine was named as a candidate in the brief, but its back-related trial was in sciatica (nerve root pain), not non-specific back pain, and Vertex has stopped that indication. Kept in the pipeline for transparency with no expected date.
- VER-01's German label is for chronic low back pain with a neuropathic component, a subset of non-specific back pain; the effect over placebo was small. The UK MHRA decision (targeted before end of 2026) is not a dated public milestone.
- The "1 in 13" ratio is our arithmetic from the GBD 2020 count and world population; GBD's age-standardised prevalence is a little lower.
