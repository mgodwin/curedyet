# Asthma: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

## Sources

- [WHO fact sheet: Asthma (28 Apr 2026)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/asthma) - about 363 million people with asthma and 442,000 deaths in 2023; most deaths in low- and lower-middle-income countries; steroid inhalers available in public primary care in fewer than half of low- and lower-middle-income countries, bronchodilators in fewer than two-thirds; inhalers let people live normal, active lives; no cure
- [CDC: Most recent national asthma data (2023)](https://www.cdc.gov/asthma-data/about/most-recent-asthma-data.html) - 27.8 million (8.4%) with current asthma; 42.3% had an attack; about 1 million ED visits and 132,000 hospital stays; 3,624 deaths; higher rates in Black Americans and people in poverty
- [GINA 2026 Summary Guide](https://ginasthma.org/2026-gina-summary-guide/) - Global Initiative for Asthma strategy: as-needed or maintenance-and-reliever ICS-formoterol as preferred track; SABA-only treatment not recommended; biologics for severe asthma
- [Bateman et al., NEJM 2018: As-needed budesonide-formoterol versus maintenance budesonide in mild asthma (SYGMA 2)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29768147/) - as-needed budesonide-formoterol non-inferior to daily budesonide for severe exacerbations, with about a quarter of the steroid dose
- [Hekking et al., J Allergy Clin Immunol 2015: The prevalence of severe refractory asthma](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25441637/) - 3.6% of adults with asthma have severe refractory asthma (Netherlands)
- [Shackleford et al., Lancet Respiratory Medicine 2025: Clinical remission attainment in severe asthma treated with biologics (meta-analysis)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39549709/) - pooled remission 38% (three-component definition) and 30% (four-component, with lung function); a minority; longer disease, severity and comorbidities are barriers
- [Menzies-Gow et al., NEJM 2021: Tezepelumab in adults and adolescents with severe, uncontrolled asthma (NAVIGATOR)](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2034975) - tezepelumab cut annual exacerbations by about 56% vs placebo
- [GSK: Exdensur (depemokimab) approved by US FDA for severe asthma (16 Dec 2025)](https://us.gsk.com/media/gctjoq5v/exdensur-fda-approval_uspr_final.pdf) - add-on for severe eosinophilic asthma from age 12; twice-yearly; SWIFT-1 and SWIFT-2 cut exacerbations 58% and 48%; 72% fewer exacerbations needing hospital or ED (pooled)
- [AstraZeneca: Positive results from Phase III KALOS and LOGOS trials for Breztri (2026)](https://www.astrazeneca.com/media-centre/press-releases/2026/positive-and-clinically-meaningful-results-from-the-phase-iii-kalos-and-logos-trials-for-breztri.html) - about 4,300 patients; 76 mL FEV1 gain; FDA approval for asthma from age 12 on 28 April 2026
- [BioPharm International: Sanofi's lunsekimig meets Phase 2 endpoints in asthma and CRSwNP (Apr 2026)](https://www.biopharminternational.com/view/sanofi-s-lunsekimig-meets-phase-2-endpoints-in-asthma-and-crswnp-showing-promise-as-a-dual-targeting-respiratory-therapy) - AIRCULES Phase 2b met primary and key secondary endpoints (exacerbations, FEV1), regardless of biomarker status; AIRLYMPUS ongoing
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06676319: AIRLYMPUS, lunsekimig in high-risk asthma](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06676319) - Phase 2, active not recruiting, primary completion 21 June 2027
- [HCPLive: Phase 2 VALIANT, verekitug reduces annual asthma flare-ups (Feb 2026)](https://www.hcplive.com/view/phase-2-valiant-verekitug-reduces-average-annual-asthma-flare-ups) - 478 adults with severe asthma; 56% reduction with 100 mg every 12 weeks, 39% with 400 mg every 24 weeks
- [Upstream Bio 8-K (Q1 2026 update)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002022626/000119312526220578/upb-ex99_1.htm) - end-of-Phase 2 FDA meeting mid-2026; Phase 3 dosing in severe asthma planned for Q1 2027
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06473779: ARRIVAL, tezepelumab and reduction of background asthma medication](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06473779) - Phase 3 open-label, active not recruiting, primary completion June 2027
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT02570984: PARK, preventing asthma in high-risk kids with omalizumab](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02570984) - active not recruiting, primary completion 31 January 2028
- [Phipatanakul et al., Contemp Clin Trials 2021: PARK design and rationale](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7887056) - about 250 children aged 24-47 months at high risk; two years of omalizumab to prevent asthma or reduce severity

## Research log

- 2026-11-05: Baseline research for the first Cure Watch record. Rated level 2 (controlled). Kept as a single record without variants.

## Open questions

- The brief cited WHO's older figures (262 million with asthma, 455,000 deaths, for 2019). WHO's fact sheet updated in April 2026 gives 363 million and 442,000 for 2023, so the newer figures are used. The jump in cases mostly reflects new estimates, not necessarily a real rise.
- Variants (mild-to-moderate vs severe) were considered and not used: both rate level 2 (controlled on ongoing treatment), and the difference is mainly in burden (cheap inhalers vs costly biologic injections). Severe asthma is a small share (about 3.6% of adults with asthma in a Dutch study, other estimates up to about 10%). If biologics produce lasting remission off treatment, a severe variant could be split out at level 3.
- Clinical remission on biologics (30-38%) is measured while treatment continues, so it is not counted as cure for level 3. Definitions vary between studies.
- Childhood asthma often eases or goes away in adolescence; this is natural remission, not a treatment effect, and is not counted.
- The access gap is stated as WHO's availability measure in public primary care; affordability data for inhaled steroids in low-income countries were not separately sourced.
- Biologic prices in the US were not sourced, so the record says only that they are costly.
