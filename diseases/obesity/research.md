## Sources

- [Healio: FDA approves orforglipron](https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20260401/fda-approves-orforglipron-an-oral-glp1-for-adults-with-obesity)
- [Retatrutide Phase 3](https://www.epocrates.com/online/article/retatrutide-heads-toward-fda-filing-after-positive-phase-3-obesity-trial-results)
- [CagriSema FDA status](https://therxindex.com/guides/cagrisema-fda-approval-status/)
- [World Obesity Atlas 2025](https://data.worldobesity.org/publications/world-obesity-atlas-2025-v7.pdf)
- [REMAIN-1 midpoint data](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/04/260423022002.htm)
- [Adams et al., Weight and Metabolic Outcomes 12 Years after Gastric Bypass, NEJM 2017](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1700459): about 27% weight loss at 12 years; diabetes remission 75%, 62% and 51% at 2, 6 and 12 years.
- [Courcoulas et al., Seven-Year Weight Trajectories and Health Outcomes in LABS-2, JAMA Surgery 2018](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamasurgery/fullarticle/2664662): bypass 28.4% weight loss at 7 years with 3.9% regain from year 3; diabetes remission 60.2% at 7 years.
- [Sjöström et al., Association of Bariatric Surgery With Long-term Remission of Type 2 Diabetes (SOS), JAMA 2014](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2014.5988): diabetes remission 30.4% at 15 years vs 6.5% in controls; follow-up attendance under 50% at 15 years.
- [Guimarães et al., How Sustained is Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass Long-term Efficacy?, Obesity Surgery 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8270797/): 281 patients at 12.2 years; 29.5% BMI under 30, mean 24.3% total weight loss, 29.9% lost under 20%, diabetes remission 54.2%.
- [Salminen et al., SLEEVEPASS 10-year results, JAMA Surgery 2022](https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/effect-of-laparoscopic-sleeve-gastrectomy-vs-roux-en-y-gastric-by-2/): excess weight loss 43.5% sleeve vs 50.7% bypass at 10 years; diabetes remission about 26% vs 33%.
- [ASMBS, Bariatric surgery procedures fall below 200,000 (May 2026)](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1126342): under 200,000 US procedures in 2024; less than 1% of eligible people have surgery in a given year.

## Research log

- 2026-11-03: Baseline research for the first Cure Watch snapshot (level, treatment, pipeline, forecasts).
- 2026-11-05: Reviewed long-term surgery evidence (Utah 12-year cohort, LABS-2, SOS, SLEEVEPASS, Portuguese 12-year cohort). About half of people with diabetes stay in remission 12 years after bypass and about 30% sit below BMI 30, so moved level 2 to 3 (cured for some); burden stays moderate because GLP-1 drugs are the typical best option, with surgery noted as heavy.
