# Common cold: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

## Sources

- [CDC: About the common cold](https://www.cdc.gov/common-cold/about/index.html) - US adults average 2-3 colds a year, children more; over 200 viruses, rhinoviruses most frequent; symptoms peak at 2-3 days; complications include ear and sinus infections, asthma attacks, bronchitis and pneumonia
- [CDC: Treating the common cold](https://www.cdc.gov/common-cold/treatment/index.html) - no cure, improves on its own; antibiotics don't work against cold viruses; no antivirals for non-flu, non-COVID cold viruses; cough and cold medicines not recommended under age 6; see a doctor if no improvement after 10 days
- [Heikkinen and Järvinen, Lancet 2003: The common cold](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(03)12162-9) - rhinoviruses cause roughly 30-50% of colds; colds usually last about 7-10 days; over 100 rhinovirus serotypes then known
- [Imperial College London: Early trials for 'common cold' vaccine offer encouraging signs (26 Aug 2026)](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/articles/medicine/nhli/2026/early-trials-for-common-cold-vaccine-offer-encouraging-signs/) - APL-10456 Phase 1, 144 healthy participants, responses to rhinovirus species A, B and C; around 180 rhinovirus strains circulate; no approved vaccines or targeted antivirals; COPD study from H1 2027
- [Apollo Therapeutics: Phase 1 APL-10456 results (25 Aug 2026)](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/apollo-therapeutics-announces-positive-immunogenicity-data-and-cross-species-activity-from-phase-1-trial-of-apl-10456-a-pan-rhinovirus-vaccine-for-the-prevention-of-copd-exacerbations) - adjuvanted recombinant protein vaccine; NCT07399132; single dose well tolerated, seroconversion and T cell responses across RV-A, B and C; Part B (two doses, age 55+) data Q4 2026; Phase 2 COPD trial H1 2027
- [GBD 2021 Upper Respiratory Infections and Otitis Media Collaborators, Lancet Infectious Diseases 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1473-3099(24)00430-4) - 12.8 billion new URI episodes worldwide in 2021; URI mortality 0.2 per 100,000
- [Jin et al., EClinicalMedicine 2021: GBD 2019 upper respiratory infections](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34386754/) - 17.2 billion URI episodes in 2019, about 43% of all incident cases in GBD 2019; highest incidence under age 5
- [University of Michigan release on Fendrick et al., Archives of Internal Medicine 2003](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/821132) - about $40 billion a year in US costs; over 100 million doctor visits ($7.7 billion); 189 million school days missed; 126 million parent workdays; over $1.1 billion on 41 million antibiotic prescriptions; $2.9 billion on OTC drugs
- [Johnston et al., BMJ 1995: viral infections in asthma exacerbations in 9-11 year olds](https://www.bmj.com/content/310/6989/1225) - viruses detected in 80-85% of asthma exacerbations in school-age children; rhinovirus most common
- [Nault et al., Cochrane 2024: Zinc for prevention and treatment of the common cold (CD014914)](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD014914/zinc-prevention-and-treatment-common-cold) - 34 trials, 8,526 people; may shorten colds by about two days, low-certainty evidence with high heterogeneity; little or no effect on preventing colds; more non-serious side effects (taste change, nausea)
- [Hemilä and Chalker, Frontiers in Medicine 2024: Shortcomings in the Cochrane review on zinc](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39478818/) - opinion arguing zinc acetate lozenges shorten colds more than the review concluded
- [Kenealy and Arroll, Cochrane 2013: Antibiotics for the common cold and acute purulent rhinitis (CD000247)](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD000247.pub3/information/en) - no evidence of benefit in children or adults; more side effects; not recommended routinely
- [Deckx et al., Cochrane 2016: Nasal decongestants in monotherapy for the common cold (CD009612)](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD009612.pub2/information) - multiple doses may give a small benefit on nasal congestion in adults, of unclear clinical relevance; no evidence in children
- [AlBalawi et al., Cochrane 2013: Intranasal ipratropium bromide for the common cold (CD008231)](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD008231_spray-containing-ipratropium-bromide-administered-nose-treat-common-cold-symptoms) - 7 trials, 2,144 people; likely eases runny nose, no effect on congestion; more nasal dryness and nosebleeds
- [De Sutter et al., Cochrane 2015: Antihistamines for the common cold (CD009345)](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD009345_antihistamines-common-cold) - small short-term benefit on severity in adults on days 1-2 (45% vs 38% felt better), none later; no evidence in children
- [Hemilä and Chalker, Cochrane 2013: Vitamin C for preventing and treating the common cold (CD000980)](https://www.cochrane.org/CD000980/vitamin-c-preventing-and-treating-common-cold) - regular use does not cut colds in the general population (RR 0.97) but halves risk under heavy physical stress; shortens colds 8% in adults, 14% in children; no consistent effect when started after symptoms
- [Jefferson et al., Cochrane 2023: Physical interventions to interrupt or reduce the spread of respiratory viruses (CD006207)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36715243/) - hand hygiene cut acute respiratory infections by 14% (RR 0.86, 9 trials, 52,105 people); masks made little or no clear difference; high risk of bias
- [Hayden et al., Clinical Infectious Diseases 2003: oral pleconaril for picornavirus colds](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7199898) - two Phase 3 trials; in 1,363 picornavirus-positive adults, illness about 1 day shorter; pleconaril induces CYP3A enzymes, affecting drugs such as oral contraceptives
- [HealthDay: FDA panel gives cold shoulder to cold drug (Mar 2002)](https://consumer.healthday.com/public-health-information-30/food-and-drug-administration-news-315/fda-panel-gives-cold-shoulder-to-cold-drug-506428.html) - FDA advisory committee voted against approving pleconaril in March 2002 on safety, including menstrual bleeding in women on oral contraceptives, and modest benefit
- [Relias Media: FDA, no approval for pleconaril](https://reliasmedia.com/articles/65465-pharmacology-watch-fda-no-approval-for-pleconaril-side-effects-cited) - FDA declined approval in 2002
- [Altesa BioSciences: Phase 2 vapendavir results at ATS 2026 (19 May 2026)](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/altesa-biosciences-presents-positive-phase-2-challenge-study-results-for-vapendavir-at-american-thoracic-society-international-conference-2026) - rhinovirus challenge in GOLD 2 COPD; median time to symptom resolution 5 vs 8.5 days; lower viral load and inflammation; Phase 2b CARDINAL, 900 participants with COPD, 49 US and UK sites, 7-day once-daily dosing, runs through Q4 2027
- [Altesa BioSciences: topline vapendavir COPD challenge results (May 2025)](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/altesa-biosciences-details-positive-topline-vapendavir-results-from-phase-2-placebo-controlled-rhinovirus-challenge-study-in-copd-patients) - first topline release; treatment started after symptoms began
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07222670: INNA-051 prophylaxis Phase 2a](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07222670) - ENA Respiratory; TLR2/6 agonist intranasal dry powder, weekly; 1,100 healthy adults 18-45; started 4 Dec 2025; primary completion est. 12 Mar 2027; endpoint includes PCR-confirmed symptomatic viral respiratory infections
- [Kyorin, Open Forum Infectious Diseases 2021: KRP-A218, a PI4KB inhibitor](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8644906/) - host-targeted, broad anti-rhinovirus activity; worked in mouse models; first-in-human study described as ongoing
- [Lee et al., Nature Communications 2016: polyvalent inactivated rhinovirus vaccine in macaques](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5036149/) - Emory; 25-valent in mice and 50-valent in rhesus macaques induced broad neutralising antibodies
- [Contagion Live: Scientists make progress on common cold vaccine (2016)](https://www.contagionlive.com/view/scientists-make-progress-on-development-of-common-cold-vaccine) - Emory team planned to pursue NIH partnership and clinical trial; first targets children with asthma and adults with COPD

## Research log

- 2026-11-05: First full record. Rated cure level 1 with light burden on symptom relief only, and prevention level 0 with heavy burden (nothing licensed to take). Found the first rhinovirus vaccine in human trials (APL-10456, Phase 1, Aug 2026) and the leading antiviral (vapendavir, Phase 2b CARDINAL in COPD).

## Open questions

- **How the cure ladder applies.** Nearly everyone recovers from a cold within about 7-10 days with no treatment, so natural recovery could be read as "cured for all". Following measles, the ladder here rates what treatment does, not what the body does: no drug clears the viruses or shortens a typical cold by more than a day or so, and remedies only ease symptoms, so it is level 1 ("treatments ease symptoms"), not 0, since Cochrane finds small real symptom benefits for some remedies. Level 4 is read as a treatment that, taken early, ends most colds within a day or two and prevents asthma and COPD flare-ups. A reviewer may prefer level 0, or may argue the self-limiting course makes a forecast for level 4 meaningless; the low odds reflect both the biology (200+ viruses) and the small room any drug has to beat natural recovery.
- **Prevention rung and burden.** Rung 0 because no vaccine or preventive drug is licensed. Hand-washing (about 14% fewer respiratory infections) and avoiding sick contacts are context, not a licensed option, and would only reach rung 1 even if counted. Burden is set to heavy to match other rung-0 records (hepatitis C, Nipah, Marburg), where nothing exists to take; it does not mean hand-washing is hard. The colour is grey either way.
- **What "strong protection" means for a disease with 200+ causes.** The prevention forecast reads it as about 85% fewer colds overall. A rhinovirus-only vaccine like APL-10456 could at most prevent the rhinovirus share (roughly 30-50% of colds), so even full success would leave the cold at rung 1 or 2. A reviewer might instead rate rhinovirus colds alone.
- **Vapendavir readout date.** CARDINAL is event-driven and "runs through Q4 2027"; no topline date is public, so `expected` is set to 2028. The challenge-study results are company-reported and not yet peer-reviewed.
- **KRP-A218 status.** The 2021 abstract described a first-in-human study, but no registry entry or later results were found; it may have been dropped.
- **Emory polyvalent vaccine.** No human trial was found in registries or news since the 2016 macaque study.
- **Economic figures are old.** The $40 billion US estimate uses 2001 survey data; no newer US-wide estimate was found. The GBD URI count includes non-cold upper respiratory infections, though colds dominate it.
- **Global access.** Colds are mild everywhere, but antibiotic overuse for colds is a global resistance problem; no clean global figure was found to cite.
