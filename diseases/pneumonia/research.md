# Pneumonia: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

## Sources

- [WHO fact sheet: The top 10 causes of death (Oct 2026)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/the-top-10-causes-of-death) - lower respiratory infections 4th leading cause of death in 2023 with 2.7 million deaths, back to 2019 level, the world's deadliest communicable disease
- [WHO fact sheet: Pneumonia in children (Nov 2022)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/pneumonia) - 740,180 under-5 deaths in 2019, 14% of under-5 deaths and 22% at ages 1-5; pneumococcus, Hib and RSV main causes; amoxicillin dispersible tablets first line, often by community health workers; oxygen for severe cases; only one third of children with pneumonia get the antibiotics they need; vaccines, breastfeeding, clean cooking, cotrimoxazole for children with HIV
- [Bender et al. (GBD 2021 LRI Collaborators), Lancet Infect Dis 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38636536/) - 344 million LRI episodes and 2.18 million non-COVID LRI deaths in 2021; 502,000 under-5 deaths, about half in low-SDI countries; S. pneumoniae the leading cause, 505,000 deaths
- [CDC FastStats: Pneumonia](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/fastats/pneumonia.htm) - 41,627 US deaths in 2024; about 1.2 million ED visits (2022)
- [Ramirez et al., Clin Infect Dis 2017: Adults hospitalized with pneumonia in the United States](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29020164/) - about 1.59 million US adult CAP hospitalisations a year; in-hospital mortality 6.5%; 13.0% at 30 days, 23.4% at 6 months, 30.6% at 1 year
- [Jain et al., NEJM 2015: Community-acquired pneumonia requiring hospitalization among US adults (CDC EPIC)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26172429/) - pathogen found in 38%: viruses 23%, bacteria 11%; rhinovirus, influenza and pneumococcus most common; 21% needed ICU, 2% died; incidence highest at 80+
- [Metlay et al., ATS/IDSA 2019 guideline: Diagnosis and treatment of adults with community-acquired pneumonia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31573350/) - outpatient amoxicillin or doxycycline; inpatient beta-lactam plus macrolide
- [Contagion Live: 2025 ATS guideline updates for community-acquired pneumonia](https://contagionlive.com/view/2025-american-thoracic-society-clinical-practice-guideline-updates-for-the-diagnosis-and-management-of-community-acquired-pneumonia) - steroids suggested for severe CAP but not for influenza; 3-5 days of antibiotics for non-severe CAP once stable, at least 5 for severe; approved independently of IDSA
- [Guideline Central: Nov 2025 ATS/IDSA CAP guideline timeline](https://www.guidelinecentral.com/insights/nov-2025-atsidsa-cap-guideline-timeline/) - IDSA did not co-endorse the 2025 update; CAPE COD vs ESCAPe and REMAP-CAP disagreement
- [Dequin et al., NEJM 2023: Hydrocortisone in severe community-acquired pneumonia (CAPE COD)](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2215145) - 28-day mortality 6.2% with hydrocortisone vs 11.9% with placebo in ICU patients
- [Horby et al. (RECOVERY), NEJM 2021: Dexamethasone in hospitalized patients with Covid-19](https://napier-repository.worktribe.com/output/2743756/dexamethasone-in-hospitalized-patients-with-covid-19-preliminary-report) - lower 28-day mortality with dexamethasone in ventilated patients (29.3% vs 41.4%) and those on oxygen (23.3% vs 26.2%), not in those without respiratory support
- [CDC: COVID-19 outpatient treatment for clinicians (Oct 2026)](https://www.cdc.gov/covid/hcp/clinical-care/outpatient-treatment.html) - nirmatrelvir-ritonavir preferred, remdesivir second, molnupiravir alternative, started within 5-7 days
- [Rudd et al., Lancet 2020: Global sepsis incidence and mortality (UPMC summary)](https://www.upmc.com/media/news/011620-rudd-global-sepsis) - 48.9 million sepsis cases and 11 million deaths in 2017; lower respiratory infection the most common underlying cause of sepsis-related death
- [WHO: Widespread resistance to common antibiotics (Oct 2025)](https://www.who.int/news/item/13-10-2025-who-warns-of-widespread-resistance-to-common-antibiotics-worldwide) - GLASS 2025: about 1 in 6 lab-confirmed bacterial infections resistant in 2023; resistance rose in over 40% of pathogen-antibiotic pairs 2018-2023; S. pneumoniae among the monitored pathogens
- [Ratner and Weiser, JCI 2006: review of Podolsky's Pneumonia before antibiotics](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/29920) - early 20th-century pneumonia case fatality estimated at 30-40%; sulfonamides in the 1930s cut it substantially
- [Annals of Internal Medicine 1941: Factors influencing the fatality rate of pneumococcic pneumonia treated with sulfonamide compounds](https://scilit.com/publications/ded213c4712b092f5da26d1b02d72c48) - 10% fatality among 800 patients treated with sulfapyridine or sulfathiazole
- [Lucero et al., Cochrane 2022 (CD004977): Pneumococcal conjugate vaccines in children under 2](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD004977_vaccines-against-overwhelming-blood-infection-due-pneumococcus-bacteria-and-lung-infection-pneumonia) - 80% efficacy against vaccine-type invasive disease; 27% against X-ray-defined pneumonia; six trials, 113,044 children
- [Pfizer: CAPiTA results published in NEJM (2015)](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/results_of_pfizer_s_community_acquired_pneumonia_immunization_trial_in_adults_capita_published_in_the_new_england_journal_of_medicine) - PCV13 in about 85,000 adults 65+: 45.6% against first vaccine-type CAP, about 75% against vaccine-type IPD; all-cause CAP 5.1% (not significant)
- [CDC: Pneumococcal vaccines (Feb 2026)](https://www.cdc.gov/pneumococcal/vaccines/index.html) - PCV15, PCV20, PCV21; all children under 5 and adults 50+ (19-49 with risk conditions); cannot prevent all cases
- [Merck: FDA approves Capvaxive (V116, PCV21) (June 2024)](https://www.merck.com/news/u-s-fda-approves-capvaxive-pneumococcal-21-valent-conjugate-vaccine-for-prevention-of-invasive-pneumococcal-disease-and-pneumococcal-pneumonia-in-adults/) - first PCV designed for adults; serotypes cause about 84% of IPD in adults 50+ vs about 52% for PCV20; pneumonia indication under accelerated approval
- [Vaxcyte 8-K, 4 Nov 2026 (Q3 results)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001649094/000164909426000051/pcvx-20261104xexx991.htm) - VAX-31 OPUS-1 adult Phase 3 (4,047 adults) met all immunogenicity endpoints, Oct 2026; OPUS-2/3 and infant Phase 2 data 1H 2027; adult BLA 1H 2028
- [Pfizer: Pfizer advances pivotal pediatric pneumococcal vaccine program (May 2026)](https://www.pfizer.com/news/press-release/press-release-detail/pfizer-advances-pivotal-pediatric-pneumococcal-vaccine) - 25vPnC Phase 3 in about 2,400 infants vs PCV20; serotype 3 response 8.8-fold higher in Phase 2; about 25% more IPD coverage than PCV20
- [Secure PCV Collaborators: 71% of world's children protected against pneumococcal disease](https://stoppneumonia.org/historic-milestone-71-of-worlds-children-protected-against-pneumococcal-disease/) - WUENIC: PCV coverage 69% in 2024, 71% in 2025
- [Every Breath Counts: Pneumonia care-seeking scorecard (2025)](https://stoppneumonia.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Pneumonia-Careseeking-Scorecard-1.pdf) - globally 38% of children with pneumonia symptoms not taken for care (2010-2023 data)
- [Every Breath Counts: Pneumonia care-seeking scorecard (2019)](https://stoppneumonia.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/Pneumonia-Care-Seeking-Scorecard.pdf) - 55% of children with symptoms taken to an appropriate provider across 60 countries (2017 data)
- [PERCH Study Group, Lancet 2019: Causes of severe pneumonia requiring hospital admission in children without HIV in Africa and Asia](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31257127/) - 30-day case fatality 6.4%; viruses 61%, bacteria 27%; RSV the largest single cause (31%)
- [Graham et al., Lancet Global Health 2025: Commission on medical oxygen security](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11865010/) - fewer than 1 in 3 people in LMICs who need oxygen receive it; closing gaps about US$6.8 billion a year
- [CDC: Flu vaccine effectiveness, past seasons estimates](https://www.cdc.gov/flu-vaccines-work/php/effectiveness-studies/past-seasons-estimates.html) - US seasonal flu vaccine effectiveness against medically attended flu roughly 30-56% since 2015
- [CDC: RSV immunization guidance for infants and young children](https://www.cdc.gov/rsv/hcp/vaccine-clinical-guidance/infants-young-children.html) - nirsevimab, clesrovimab and maternal vaccine; high efficacy against RSV hospitalisation
- [CIDRAP: 2025-26 COVID vaccine cuts emergency and urgent care visits by half](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/covid-19/study-suggests-2025-26-covid-vaccine-cuts-emergency-urgent-care-visits-half) - CDC-led study: about 53-55% effectiveness against hospitalisation in adults
- [Contagion Live: Zelicapavir in RSV](https://www.contagionlive.com/view/novel-rsv-therapy-addressing-a-clinical-need) - missed primary symptom endpoint in high-risk adults but lowered hospitalisation; paediatric Phase 2b in 2027

## Research log

- 2026-11-05: Baseline research for the first Cure Watch record. Split into community-acquired bacterial, viral and childhood (low- and middle-income settings) variants because treatment, outlook and prevention differ sharply. Rated the disease at 3 overall: bacterial at 4, viral at 1 (matching the flu and RSV pages), childhood in low-income settings at 3 because of access.

## Open questions

- The brief described lower respiratory infections as WHO's 5th leading cause of death (~2.5 million, 2021 data); WHO's October 2026 update ranks them 4th with 2.7 million deaths in 2023, which is what the record uses.
- Bacterial level 4 rests on judgement: no source gives a cure rate for all bacterial CAP including outpatients. Hospitalised adults have about 13% 30-day mortality (all causes of CAP mixed), just under the 85% line for that group alone; outpatient deaths are rare but not well quantified in the sources found. Could be argued as 3 for hospitalised older adults.
- Reached year 1945 for bacterial pneumonia is approximate; historical case-fatality figures vary by era and population, and one historical series found 17% mortality in a city hospital in 1952-62.
- Childhood variant departs from the rich-country rule on purpose; rating 3 on access grounds and burden heavy for poor access to a cheap drug are judgement calls. Most childhood pneumonia episodes resolve even in poor countries (about 0.5 million deaths among very many episodes), so the variant is really about the severe cases and the children who never reach care.
- No good source found for overall case fatality of viral versus bacterial pneumonia in rich countries; EPIC's 2% in-hospital mortality excluded severely immunocompromised patients and is lower than the Louisville figure.
- Prevention rungs: vaccines are strong against specific germs (vaccine-type pneumococcus, Hib, RSV in infants) but weak against all-cause pneumonia; rungs were set on all-cause pneumonia for bacterial and childhood, and on hospitalisation by the main viruses for viral.
- S. pneumoniae-specific resistance rates from GLASS 2025 were not extracted.
