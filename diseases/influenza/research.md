# Flu (seasonal influenza): research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

## Sources

- [WHO fact sheet: Influenza (seasonal) (28 Feb 2025)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/influenza-(seasonal)) - about 1 billion cases a year, 3-5 million severe cases, 290,000-650,000 respiratory deaths; 99% of deaths in under-5s with flu-related lower respiratory infection are in developing countries; antivirals advised for high-risk or severe patients; annual vaccination for priority groups; inactivated, recombinant and live nasal vaccines
- [CDC FluView, week 20 ending 23 May 2026](https://www.cdc.gov/fluview/surveillance/2026-week-20.html) - final full weekly report of 2025-26; at least 32 million illnesses, 390,000 hospitalisations and 24,000 deaths; 174 paediatric deaths; moderate severity overall, high in children
- [CDC: About influenza (key facts)](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/about/index.html) - about 8% of the US population gets sick from flu each season, range 3% to 11% depending on season
- [CDC: Influenza antiviral medications, summary for clinicians](https://www.cdc.gov/flu/hcp/antivirals/summary-clinicians.html) - four FDA-approved antivirals: oseltamivir (oral, 5 days), zanamivir (inhaled, 5 days, age 7+), peramivir (single IV dose, 6 months+), baloxavir (single oral dose, age 5+, also post-exposure prophylaxis); early treatment shortens fever and illness; may reduce some complications; observational studies of hospitalised patients mixed, some show fewer deaths; treat hospitalised, severe and high-risk patients; side effects (nausea and vomiting with oseltamivir, bronchospasm with zanamivir)
- [Jefferson et al., Cochrane 2014: Neuraminidase inhibitors for preventing and treating influenza (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24718923/) - used full clinical study reports; oseltamivir shortened symptoms by 16.8 hours in adults; no significant effect on hospitalisation; reduced only unverified self-reported pneumonia; more nausea and vomiting
- [Dobson et al., Lancet 2015: Oseltamivir treatment for influenza in adults, meta-analysis of randomised trials](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)62449-1) - Roche-funded individual-patient analysis; in people with confirmed flu, faster symptom relief and fewer lower respiratory complications and hospital admissions
- [ECDC expert opinion on neuraminidase inhibitors (Eurosurveillance, PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4814868/) - weighs the Jefferson and Dobson reviews; trials underpowered for severe outcomes; analyses suggest some benefit on complications and hospitalisation
- [Hanula et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2023: Oseltamivir to prevent hospitalisation in outpatients with influenza](https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2023.0699) - 15 randomised trials, 6,295 people; no significant reduction in hospitalisation overall or in higher-risk groups; more nausea and vomiting
- [WHO: Clinical practice guidelines for influenza, executive summary (Sep 2024)](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/health-care-readiness/clinical-guidelines-for-influenza---executive-summary.pdf) - conditional recommendation for baloxavir in non-severe flu at high risk of severe disease and against it at low risk; strong recommendation against oseltamivir and zanamivir for non-severe flu; conditional recommendation for oseltamivir in severe flu; against corticosteroids, macrolides and plasma; baloxavir or oseltamivir for high-risk exposed people
- [Ison et al., Lancet Infectious Diseases 2020: CAPSTONE-2, baloxavir in high-risk outpatients (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32526195/) - 2,184 high-risk patients; symptoms improved in 73.2 hours vs 102.3 on placebo (29.1 hours faster) and 81.0 on oseltamivir; viral shedding stopped faster; reduced-susceptibility variants in 5%; Shionogi-funded
- [Roche: NEJM publishes CENTERSTONE data on baloxavir and transmission (25 Apr 2025)](https://www.roche.com/media/releases/med-cor-2025-04-25) - Monto et al., NEJM 2025; household transmission 9.5% vs 13.4% (adjusted odds ratio 0.68); symptomatic transmission not significant; resistance in 7.2% of treated index patients; submitted to FDA and EMA
- [Kumar et al., Lancet Infectious Diseases 2022: FLAGSTONE (Medical Xpress summary)](https://medicalxpress.com/news/2022-02-drug-combination-outcomes-influenza.pdf) - 322 hospitalised patients with severe flu; adding baloxavir to a neuraminidase inhibitor did not improve time to clinical improvement
- [China Daily: China approves novel homegrown flu drug (Jun 2025)](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202506/11/WS684965ea498eec7e1f73987f/china-approves-novel-homegrown-flu-drug.html) - onradivir, a PB2 cap-binding inhibitor for influenza A, approved in China
- [FilingReader: Zhongsheng children's onradivir enters priority review (2 Jun 2026)](https://filingreader.com/news-wire/shenzhen/2026-06-02/zhongsheng-pharmaceutical-flu-drug-for-children-enters-priority-review) - granules for ages 2-12; 120-child Phase 3 against oseltamivir met its goals
- [CDC: Past seasons' vaccine effectiveness estimates](https://www.cdc.gov/flu-vaccines-work/php/effectiveness-studies/past-seasons-estimates.html) - adjusted overall VE against medically attended flu: 48% (2015-16), 40%, 38%, 29%, 39%, not estimated (2020-21), 36%, 30%, 44%, 56% (2024-25), 36% (2025-26, preliminary)
- [CDC MMWR, 12 Mar 2026: Interim 2025-26 vaccine effectiveness](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7509a2.htm) - children 38-41% outpatient, 41% hospitalisation; adults 22-34% outpatient, 30% hospitalisation; flu B 63% in adults; lower because H3N2 subclade K drifted from the vaccine strain
- [CDC press release: Preference for specific flu vaccines for adults 65+ (30 Jun 2022)](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/media/releases/2022/s0630-seniors-flu.html) - high-dose, recombinant and adjuvanted vaccines preferentially recommended for 65+; standard dose if none available
- [AstraZeneca: FluMist Home launch (15 Aug 2025)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250815310613/en) - FDA approved self- and caregiver administration in Sep 2024; home delivery for the 2025-26 season in 34 states; self-administration ages 18-49, caregivers for ages 2-17
- [BioPharma Dive: FDA approves Moderna's mRNA flu vaccine (Aug 2026)](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/moderna-fda-approve-mflusiva-seasonal-influenza/826864/) - mFlusiva (mRNA-1010) approved 5 Aug 2026 for adults 50+; 27% fewer flu-like illnesses than a standard-dose shot (NEJM); 65+ under accelerated approval pending a two-season confirmatory study; available for 2026-27
- [Pharmacy Times: FDA declines to review mRNA-1010 (Feb 2026)](https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-declines-to-review-moderna-s-mrna-1010-flu-vaccine-application-citing-comparator-concerns) - initial refusal to file over the choice of comparator, later reversed
- [Fierce Biotech: Pfizer details Phase 3 mRNA flu vaccine data](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/pfizer-details-phase-3-data-mrna-flu-vaccine) - modRNA vaccine, adults 18-64, relative efficacy 34.5% vs licensed inactivated vaccine (NEJM 2025)
- [Contagion Live: Pfizer's mRNA flu vaccine shows superior efficacy (21 Nov 2025)](https://www.contagionlive.com/view/pfizer-s-mrna-influenza-vaccine-shows-superior-efficacy-to-inactivated-vaccine-in-phase-3-trial) - end-of-season relative efficacy 28.7%; Pfizer in discussion with health authorities on licensure, no timeline
- [GSK: mRNA seasonal flu vaccine to Phase III after positive Phase II data (Sep 2026)](https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/media/press-releases/gsk-to-advance-mrna-seasonal-flu-vaccine-candidate-to-phase-iii-following-positive-phase-ii-data/) - FLUm3HA.b-3NA chosen after 971-person Flu-028 Phase 2; Phase 3 efficacy trial planned from Sep 2026; FDA Fast Track in Jul 2026
- [Contagion Live: Cidara's CD388 shows up to 76% protection in Phase 2b NAVIGATE (23 Jun 2025)](https://www.contagionlive.com/view/cidara-s-cd388-demonstrates-up-to-76-protection-against-influenza-in-phase-2b-navigate-trial) - over 5,000 healthy adults 18-64; single dose; 76.1% at 450 mg, lower at smaller doses; protection over the season; company-reported, not yet peer-reviewed
- [Merck: Merck to acquire Cidara Therapeutics (Nov 2025)](https://www.merck.com/news/merck-to-acquire-cidara-therapeutics-inc-diversifying-its-portfolio-to-include-late-phase-antiviral-agent/) - about $9.2 billion; CD388 a long-acting, strain-agnostic drug-Fc conjugate for flu prevention; Breakthrough Therapy designation
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07159763: ANCHOR, MK-1406/CD388 for prevention of influenza](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07159763) - Phase 3, placebo-controlled, high-risk adults and adolescents; started 25 Sep 2025; estimated 10,000 participants; US, UK, Japan, Argentina, Australia, South Africa; estimated primary completion 30 Jun 2027 (registry checked 5 Nov 2026)

- [PharmaTimes: Osivax reports Phase 2b data for OVX836 (Oct 2026)](https://pharmatimes.com/news/osivax-reports-phase-2b-data-for-ovx836/) - 2,850 healthy adults in Europe; 24% efficacy point estimate vs 50% target, not significant; combination studies with seasonal vaccines from Q4 2027
- [SynBioBeta: Centivax fully enrols universal flu vaccine trial (2026)](https://www.synbiobeta.com/read/centivax-fully-enrolls-180-subject-universal-flu-vaccine-trial-two-months-after-first-dose) - Centi-Flu 01 Phase 1a, 180 participants, Australia; first dosed Feb 2026; tested against a 24-strain panel; first data expected within the year

## Research log

- 2026-11-05: First full record. Rated cure level 1 with light burden, and prevention level 1 with moderate burden. Pandemic bird flu stays on the separate h5n1 record.

## Open questions

- **How the cure ladder applies.** Following measles and the common cold, the ladder rates what treatment does, not natural recovery: almost everyone recovers, but antivirals only shorten illness by about a day and their effect on hospital stays and deaths is unproven in trials, so level 1. Flu differs from the cold in that real antivirals exist and observational data suggest benefit in hospitalised patients; a reviewer could argue for level 2, but "near-normal life on ongoing treatment" does not fit an acute infection.
- **The Cochrane debate.** Cochrane (2014) and Hanula et al. (2023) found no clear effect on hospitalisation; Dobson et al. (2015, Roche-funded) found fewer complications and admissions in confirmed flu. Trials enrolled mostly healthy outpatients and were not powered for severe outcomes. WHO (2024) went further than CDC, advising against oseltamivir for non-severe flu, while CDC still recommends treating all high-risk patients. Dobson's exact admission figures were not checked against the full paper here.
- **Prevention rung 1 vs 2.** The brief's "about 40-60% in a good match year" sits on the 50% line between rungs 1 and 2. CDC's overall VE ranged 29-56% across the last ten estimated seasons, with only 2024-25 above 50% and 2025-26 at 36%, so rung 1 is used for the typical season. Protection against hospitalisation in children is sometimes higher. CD388 (76% in Phase 2b) could lift flu to rung 2 if ANCHOR confirms it and it is approved.
- **Prevention burden.** Set to moderate (repeat yearly doses) rather than light, even though each shot is cheap and mild.
- **ANCHOR timing.** Merck's Q3 2026 deck says primary completion "in June" without a year; the registry gives 30 Jun 2027 and 10,000 participants, while earlier releases said 6,000 enrolled in the Northern Hemisphere and Merck said in Jan 2026 it expected a January 2027 completion. `expected` is set to 2027.
- **Centi-Flu 01 data.** Expected "within the year" per early-2026 reports; no result was found by 5 Nov 2026, so `expected: 2026-12` may slip.
- **Baloxavir transmission label.** CENTERSTONE results were submitted to FDA and EMA in 2025; no decision on a transmission claim was found.
- **US burden figures.** The 2025-26 numbers are CDC's preliminary "at least" lower bounds; CDC's burden page with ranges could not be loaded directly. The 2026-27 season has just begun.
- **8% figure.** CDC's key-facts page gives about 8% (3-11%); the range traces to a Clinical Infectious Diseases modelling paper. It counts symptomatic illness, not all infections.
- **AI impact.** Rated low: no AI-discovered flu drug or vaccine is in late trials found here. Computational antigen design (Centivax, DIOSynVax) and machine-learning strain forecasting may change this.
