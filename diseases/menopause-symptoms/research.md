# Menopause symptoms: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

## Sources

- [WHO fact sheet: Menopause (16 Oct 2024)](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/menopause) - menopause as one point in a continuum of life stages; natural menopause usually between 45 and 55; menopause before 40 called premature; bone loss raises osteoporosis and fracture rates; women's heart-disease advantage fades after estrogen falls; access and training gaps in most countries
- [Delanerolle et al., Lancet Global Health 2025: Menopause, a global health and wellbeing issue that needs urgent attention](https://doi.org/10.1016/S2214-109X(24)00528-X) - over 1.2 billion women menopausal or postmenopausal by 2030 (WHO projection)
- [Nappi et al., Menopause 2021: Global cross-sectional survey of women with vasomotor symptoms (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8746897/) - up to 80% of women have VMS in the transition; moderate-to-severe VMS in 34% (US), 40% (Europe), 16% (Japan) of postmenopausal women aged 40-65; sleep difficulty in 60-69%
- [Mayo Clinic News Network: study puts price tag on cost of menopause symptoms in the workplace (Apr 2023)](https://newsnetwork.mayoclinic.org/discussion/mayo-clinic-study-puts-price-tag-on-cost-of-menopause-symptoms-for-women-in-the-workplace/) - about $1.8B a year in US lost work time, $26.6B including medical costs; 11% missed work in the past year because of symptoms
- [Avis et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2015: Duration of menopausal vasomotor symptoms (SWAN, PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25686030/) - median total duration of frequent VMS 7.4 years; more than 11.8 years when they began pre- or early perimenopause
- [MacLennan et al., Cochrane 2004: Oral oestrogen and combined therapy versus placebo for hot flushes (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15495039/) - 24 trials, 3,329 women; 75% reduction in weekly hot flush frequency versus placebo
- [The 2022 hormone therapy position statement of The North American Menopause Society (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35797481/) - hormone therapy most effective for VMS and GSM and prevents bone loss; favourable benefit-risk under 60 or within 10 years of menopause; less favourable later because of heart disease, stroke, clot and dementia risks
- [NAMS press release: 2023 Nonhormone Therapy Position Statement (1 Jun 2023)](https://menopause.org/wp-content/uploads/press-release/nonhormone-therapy-position-statement-release.pdf) - recommended non-hormone options: CBT, clinical hypnosis, weight loss, stellate ganglion block, SSRIs/SNRIs, gabapentin, oxybutynin, fezolinetant; many supplements and others not recommended
- [FDA: HHS removes misleading FDA warnings on hormone replacement therapy (10 Nov 2025)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/hhs-advances-womens-health-removes-misleading-fda-warnings-hormone-replacement-therapy) - FDA starts removing boxed warnings on heart disease, breast cancer and probable dementia; keeps endometrial cancer boxed warning for systemic estrogen-alone; label to recommend starting within 10 years of menopause or before 60; WHI participants averaged 63
- [FDA: FDA approves labeling changes to menopausal hormone therapy products (12 Feb 2026)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-labeling-changes-menopausal-hormone-therapy-products) - first 6 revised labels across 4 categories including topical vaginal estrogen; 29 companies submitted changes
- [Contemporary OB/GYN: FDA removes boxed warnings from 3 more menopausal hormone therapy products (30 Oct 2026)](https://www.contemporaryobgyn.net/view/fda-removes-boxed-warnings-from-3-more-menopausal-hormone-therapy-products) - further label approvals; more expected
- [FDA: Novel drug to treat moderate to severe hot flashes caused by menopause (May 2023)](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-novel-drug-treat-moderate-severe-hot-flashes-caused-menopause) - fezolinetant (Veozah) approval
- [FDA Drug Safety Communication: liver injury with Veozah (fezolinetant), updated Dec 2024](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-communications/fda-adds-warning-about-rare-occurrence-serious-liver-injury-use-veozah-fezolinetant-hot-flashes-due) - rare serious liver injury; boxed warning added December 2024; liver tests before and during treatment
- [Bayer: Lynkuet (elinzanetant) approved in the US (Oct 2025)](https://www.bayer.com/media/en-us/bayers-lynkuet-elinzanetant-approved-in-the-us-for-treatment-of-moderate-to-severe-vasomotor-symptoms-due-to-menopause/) - approved Oct 24, 2025; dual NK1/NK3 blocker taken at bedtime; OASIS 1-3 trials
- [FDA risk assessment review, NDA 219469 (elinzanetant)](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2025/219469Orig1s000RiskR.pdf) - liver warning in Warnings and Precautions with tests at baseline and 3 months; no boxed warning judged necessary; fezolinetant boxed warning history
- [Targeted Oncology: elinzanetant granted priority review for VMS from breast cancer endocrine therapy (28 Sep 2026)](https://www.targetedonc.com/view/elinzanetant-priority-review-vms-breast-cancer-endocrine-therapy) - OASIS-4, 474 patients; fewer moderate-to-severe hot flashes at weeks 4 and 12; EU approved both uses Nov 2025; no decision date published
- [EMA: Fylrevy (estetrol) EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/fylrevy) - EU authorisation 26 Mar 2026 as menopausal hormone therapy; two trials of 1,225 women reduced hot flush number and severity
- [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05836025: Effect of rapamycin in ovarian aging](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05836025) - Phase 2, 50 women, 5 mg weekly for 12 weeks vs placebo; primary completion Oct 2025, study completion estimated Sep 2026
- [Columbia OB/GYN 2024 annual report: clinical trial explores delaying menopause (VIBRANT)](https://reports.obgyn.columbia.edu/2024-annual-report/ground-breaking-clinical-trial-explores-delaying-menopause) - early unpublished results suggest rapamycin may slow ovarian aging by about 20%; plans for a larger multicenter trial
- [The 2020 genitourinary syndrome of menopause position statement of NAMS (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32852449/) - GSM affects about 27-84% of postmenopausal women, likely underdiagnosed and undertreated; moisturisers for mild symptoms; low-dose vaginal estrogen, vaginal DHEA, systemic estrogen and ospemifene effective for moderate to severe; no progestogen needed; endometrial safety not studied beyond 1 year; insufficient evidence on laser
- [ESHRE, ASRM, CREWHIRL and IMS 2024 evidence-based guideline: premature ovarian insufficiency (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39647506/) - POI prevalence 3.5% in new data; effects on fertility, bone, heart and brain health; hormone therapy mitigates some
- [ESHRE 2015 guideline summary: management of women with premature ovarian insufficiency](https://www.eshre.eu/-/media/sitecore-files/Guidelines/POI/ESHRE-guideline_POI-2015_summary_01122015.pdf) - prevalence about 1%; HRT for bone and heart health continued at least until average age of natural menopause; no breast cancer rise before that age; small chance of spontaneous pregnancy; egg donation established option

## Research log

- 2026-11-05: New record with three variants: hot flashes and night sweats (level 2, moderate), genitourinary syndrome of menopause (level 2, light) and menopause before 40 (level 2, moderate). Disease-level forecast follows the vasomotor variant. Bone loss is left to the osteoporosis record. No prevention block, since this isn't an infection.

## Open questions

- **Framing.** Menopause itself is not rated or treated as a disease. The ladder is applied to its symptoms and effects, asking how well they can be relieved. "Cured" can't mean "menopause doesn't happen", so for this record we read level 4 as lasting relief for most women without ongoing treatment, or a safe way to delay ovarian aging. Neither exists, so the forecasts are long and wide.
- **Why every variant is "Controlled" (2).** Hormone therapy cuts hot flashes by about 75% versus placebo, the non-hormonal pills work for many who can't or won't use hormones, and vaginal treatments relieve most GSM, so near-normal life on treatment is realistic for most women. But relief lasts only while treatment continues: GSM comes back when it stops, and hot flashes return if they haven't yet faded. Hot flashes do often fade on their own over years, which makes the vasomotor variant feel closer to "cured by waiting", but that is the natural course, not a cure; we kept it at 2 rather than inventing a higher rung. Level 1 would understate how well treatment works.
- **GSM burden light, not moderate.** Low-dose vaginal estrogen is used indefinitely, which METHODOLOGY usually counts as moderate. We rated it light because it is a simple local treatment a few times a week with minimal body-wide effect, few side effects and cheap generics, similar to how glasses for presbyopia are rated light. Brand-name US prices can be high, and dosing frequency comes from general practice rather than a cited label; a reviewer could reasonably pick moderate.
- **Vasomotor burden moderate.** Hormone therapy itself is often easy to take, but it carries small age-dependent risks and needs periodic review, and fezolinetant needs liver testing. Moderate seemed fairest across options.
- **FDA boxed-warning status (as of 2026-11-05).** The November 2025 announcement started the process; 6 labels were approved in February 2026 and 3 more in October 2026, with more pending. Not every product's label has changed yet, and the endometrial cancer boxed warning stays for systemic estrogen-alone products. Some clinicians criticised the process; we describe the change factually without taking a side.
- **Elinzanetant liver warning.** The FDA review says labeling carries a liver warning with tests at baseline and 3 months but no boxed warning. We didn't read the final printed label directly.
- **POI prevalence.** The 2015 ESHRE guideline says about 1%; the 2024 guideline cites newer data at 3.5%, partly from different definitions and the one-test diagnosis. We show both. Bone and heart protection from hormone therapy in POI comes from guideline statements, which rest partly on observational data.
- **Elinzanetant breast-cancer decision date** (2027-03) is an estimate from priority-review timing; no date was published.
- **VIBRANT results** are unpublished; the ~20% figure comes from Columbia's own report and is a pilot signal on markers of ovarian aging, not a shown delay in menopause. Sources disagree on enrolment details; we use the registry's 50.
- **Toll ratio** uses a company-funded survey (Astellas authors) because it gives a clear moderate-to-severe share by region; prevalence varies a lot by country and survey method.
- **Mood.** Sleep problems are well documented (60-69% in the Nappi survey); we mention mood only briefly, as a common change around menopause, and didn't add a sourced depression-risk figure.
- **Osteoporosis link.** The record points to a separate osteoporosis record, which doesn't yet exist on this branch; the bone-loss statements here rely on WHO and the NAMS 2022 statement.
