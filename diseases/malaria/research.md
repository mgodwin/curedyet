# Malaria: research

Evidence behind this disease's record. Every claim in disease.yaml should trace back to a source listed here.

## Sources

- [WHO World Malaria Report 2025](https://www.who.int/teams/global-malaria-programme/reports/world-malaria-report-2025)
- [EDCTP: KALUMA trial results](https://www.edctp.org/?p=6894)
- [Health Policy Watch: GanLum Phase 3](https://healthpolicy-watch.news/new-malaria-drug-candidate-exceeds-cure-rate-for-standard-acts-in-phase-3-trial/)
- [JHU media briefing on malaria vaccines (Apr 2026)](https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2026/media-briefing-malaria-vaccines-trends-and-whats-ahead)
- [Gene drive mosquitoes set for 2029 release (Nation)](https://www.nation.africa/kenya/health/-gene-drive-mosquitoes-set-for-2029-release-to-fight-malaria-in-east-africa-5160658)
- [Metaculus malaria mortality question](https://metaculus.com/questions/1332/will-global-malaria-mortality-rates-be-reduced-by-90-when-compared-with-2015-rates-by-2030)
- [Datoo et al., Lancet 2024: R21/Matrix-M phase 3 (PubMed)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38310910/) - 12-month efficacy 75% at seasonal sites, 68% at standard sites; 3 doses plus booster
- [WHO Q&A: Malaria vaccines (RTS,S and R21)](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/q-a-on-rts-s-malaria-vaccine) - Both vaccines cut cases over 50% in year one, about 75% where seasonal and given with SMC; 13% drop in child deaths in pilots; 4-dose schedule from 5 months; 25 African countries
- [Gavi: malaria vaccine programme](https://www.gavi.org/our-work/vaccine-portfolio/malaria) - 25 countries introduced; tens of millions of doses delivered; funding constraints
- [Cairns et al., PLoS Med 2021: SMC effectiveness at scale](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8457484/) - SMC 88% protective within 28 days of a dose, 61% at 29-42 days
- [NIH: antibody reduces risk of malaria in children (L9LS, Mali)](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/antibody-reduces-risk-malaria-children) - L9LS phase 2: 77% (300 mg) and 67% (150 mg) protection in children 6-10
- [LSTM: safety and efficacy of L9LS in Kenyan children](https://research.lstmed.ac.uk/en/publications/safety-and-efficacy-of-the-monoclonal-antibody-l9ls-for-malaria-p/) - Lower efficacy (about 43%) in young children with intense transmission
